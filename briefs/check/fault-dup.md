# Duplicate check — dup-anon.txt (24 items, C1–C15 / S1–S9)

## Verdict

**No duplicate pairs found.** All 24 items are about 24 different schemes, reliefs or
rules. I could not find two items that make substantially the same point, in either
direction across the language split (RO: C1–C5, S1–S3 / EN: C6–C15, S4–S9).

This is the result most likely to be wrong, so the full working is below: first what
each item is actually about, then every pair I weighed and why I rejected it.

## Pairs considered and rejected

**Hung on the same date — the closest thing to a real finding**

- **C3 / C11** (cross-language). Both use the 31 January Self Assessment deadline as
  the hook. Rejected: C3's point *is* the deadline — that the money has to arrive, so
  Bacs on 30 January is too late. C11's point is the Gift Aid carry-back claim, which
  you lose if the return is late. Different action, different audience, no shared
  threshold. Only one of the two is a post *about* 31 January.
- **C11 / C12**. Both carry "31 Jan 2027" as the pull-quote figure for the 2025-26
  return. Rejected: this is exactly two different rules sharing a date — a Gift Aid
  carry-back box versus a £100-per-partner late filing penalty.
- **C3 / C12**. Same date, but payment arriving versus a filing penalty. Rejected.
- Worth flagging editorially rather than as a duplicate: C3, C11 and C12 are three
  items hung on the same 31 January pivot. Not duplicates, but they should not be
  scheduled in the same week.

**Council tax — different schemes that happen to share the tax**

- **S4 / S5**. One is a £100 energy payment routed through bands A–D, the other is the
  25% single person discount. Rejected: different schemes, different money, different
  qualifying test.
- **C7 / S4**, **C7 / S5**. C7 is a surcharge added to the bill on £2m homes from 2028;
  S4 and S5 take money off an ordinary bill now. Rejected: opposite direction, no
  shared figure.

**25% government top-ups**

- **C5 / S8**. Both are "the state adds 25% to what you put in" and both involve HMRC
  money landing in an account. Rejected: Tax-Free Childcare (£2 per £8, capped £500 a
  quarter, paid to a registered provider, lost if you claim Universal Credit) and the
  Lifetime ISA (£1,000 on £4,000, provider claims it, closed to new savers at 40) share
  only the arithmetic. No shared threshold, deadline or advice.

**Savings and accounts**

- **S1 / S8**. Both "free money sitting in a savings account". Rejected: S1 is finding an
  existing matured Child Trust Fund, S8 is a bonus on new contributions.
- **S1 / S9**, **S8 / S9**. Rejected: S9 is income tax on interest, not an account type.
- **S2 / S9**. Both are "more income you can have without tax". Rejected: a disability
  allowance added to the Personal Allowance versus the starting rate band for savings
  interest — different mechanism, different figure, different claimant.

**Disability and care**

- **C4 / C6** (both of them, plus S2 as a third). C4 is a weekly benefit for care needs
  after State Pension age; C6 is a capital grant from the council for adaptations; S2 is
  a tax allowance for certified sight loss. Rejected: all three are disability-adjacent
  and all three say "savings/income are or are not counted", but they are three
  unrelated entitlements.
- **C4 / C9**. Both reach the same people (State Pension age, long-term condition).
  Rejected: C9 is a free register of practical help and says outright that it puts no
  money on your bill.

**Energy**

- **C9 / S4** (cross-language set aside — both EN). Rejected: a support register versus a
  £100 payment. They are near-opposites on the key point of whether money changes hands.

**Benefits with a £500 headline**

- **C5 / S6**. Both put £500 in front of a parent. Rejected: £500 a quarter of childcare
  top-up versus a one-off £500 maternity grant on form SF100. C5 excludes Universal
  Credit claimants; S6 requires them.

**Employer exemptions with the same structural warning**

- **C1 / C2**. Both say the figure is a limit and not an allowance, and both say that
  going a pound over taxes the whole amount rather than the excess. Rejected: that is a
  shared framing applied to two different exemptions (£150 per head annual function
  versus £50 trivial benefits), with different limits, different claimants and no
  shared date.

**Property transactions**

- **C7 / C8**. Both concern homes, values and "bands". Rejected: a council tax surcharge
  from 2028 versus the SDLT rate table on a second property today.

**VAT**

- **C10 / C13**. Rejected: the margin scheme on second-hand stock versus late submission
  penalty points. Both VAT, nothing else shared.

**Late filing penalties**

- **C12 / C13**. Both are "file late, HMRC charges you". Rejected: £100 per partner under
  Self Assessment versus a four-point threshold then £200 under the VAT regime. Different
  regime, different trigger, different amount.

**Starting out / expenses**

- **C14 / C15**. Both address a business in its first months. Rejected: a three-month
  registration duty versus a seven-year look-back on pre-trading spend.
- **C15 / S3**. Both are "expenses you can claim". Rejected: S3 is an employee flat-rate
  deduction and explicitly excludes the self-employed; C15 is for a new trade. They are
  complementary, not overlapping.

## Note on method

I compared every item against every other across the language split rather than within
it, working from the scheme each item describes rather than its wording, so that a
Romanian item and an English item about the same thing would collide. The set resolves
to 24 distinct schemes, which is why nothing collides. The repeated patterns in the set
are structural (a threshold, a "who is excluded" panel, a "claim it yourself versus
automatic" line) rather than substantive, and structure repeating across items is the
format working as intended, not duplication.
