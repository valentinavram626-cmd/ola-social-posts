### What the review changed before publication

* **Check 2 (CAP 3.7), unevidenced claims.** Removed: a Marriage Allowance
  take-up claim, in both its original and its reworded form; "the largest
  exclusion / most often misunderstood" in Romanian, English and the Google
  Business frame; "the part most people get wrong" on the Romanian VAT caption;
  "the most common cash-flow surprise" on payments on account; "where most of
  the confusion sits" on MTD. The firm holds evidence for none of them.
* **Check 2, guarantee of outcome.** Marriage Allowance stated a flat "£252"
  where GOV.UK says "up to £252". Corrected on the cards, the Google Business
  frame and every caption. This was the block.
* **Check 1, a heading that inverted its own rule.** The Marriage Allowance card
  carrying £12,570 was headed "Cine NU se califică / Who does not qualify"
  directly above the *qualifying* conditions, so the frame read as "under
  £12,570 means you do not qualify". Now "Condiții și excluderi / Conditions and
  exclusions".
* **Check 4, tax-evasion adjacency.** The VAT call to action read "message us
  before you cross it, not after", which is equally readable as an offer to keep
  a business under the threshold. Reworded on the card and on the closing card.
* **Check 1, VAT deregistration.** The card read as a backward-looking
  entitlement. VAT Notice 700/11 section 3.2 makes it forward-looking: HMRC must
  be satisfied that taxable turnover *in the next 12 months* will not exceed the
  limit. Corrected on the cards and in the captions.
* **Check 1, a wrong word in Romanian.** A caption said a business "înregistrată"
  (registered) outside the UK has no threshold. The test is *established*. A
  Romanian company with a UK fixed establishment is UK-established and the
  £90,000 threshold does apply to it. Corrected to "stabilită", and the
  zero-rated exception added.
* **Check 6, implied endorsement.** The Marriage Allowance kicker paired the Ola
  wordmark with "HMRC" on every frame. Now "· gov.uk". Ola is supervised by HMRC
  for anti-money-laundering only.
* **Check 9, the disclaimer.** "Informații generale. Pentru situația ta,
  vorbește cu un contabil." was absent from every item. This was a template
  defect, not five drafting slips: the card template had no slot for it. A slot
  was added to the closing card and the single-frame layout, and the line now
  appears on the closing card of every carousel and at the foot of every
  caption, in both languages.
* **Check 3, prices.** None of the four quotes a price, so the VAT-inclusive
  rule is not engaged. Nothing to reconcile against the published fees.
* **Check 5, confidentiality.** Clean. No names, handles, enquiry details or
  identifiable circumstances in any item.

### `winter-fuel-35000` — not published, and why

Stopped by the fact check and by the independent verifier, and left stopped on a
compliance condition:

1. **Two wrong figures.** The lead card said "£200–£300 for pensioners". GOV.UK's
   own overview says "between £100 and £300" — £200–£300 is the living-alone
   rate only, and a pensioner living with an eligible partner gets £100 or £150.
   A second card gave "£200 for those born on or before 27 June 1960" when £200
   is the band from 28 September 1946; someone born earlier gets £300.
2. **An exclusion list the firm cannot complete.** GOV.UK's eligibility list
   includes people whose leave to enter says they cannot claim public funds.
   Publishing that line would take an unauthorised firm into no-recourse-to-
   public-funds territory with precisely the audience it markets to; omitting it
   while presenting the list as complete misleads the readers it most affects.
   Both the reviewer and this log take the same view: the line stays out, and so
   does the carousel.
3. **A process condition that does not yet exist.** The item was built to draw
   WhatsApp enquiries about a benefit whose statutory gate includes recourse to
   public funds. Some replies would volunteer immigration status and ask whether
   the sender qualifies. Answering that is regulated activity under the
   Immigration Advice Authority and Ola is not authorised; the penalty reaches
   £15,000. A written inbox rule — answer the income and tax-code half, never the
   status half, route status questions to GOV.UK and to an authorised adviser —
   needs to exist before this subject is published. It is a decision for the
   principal, not something a scheduled run can create for itself.

A render fault was also open on the Romanian 9:16 lead frame.

### Standing items raised for the principal

1. The disclaimer gap was a template defect and would have recurred every week.
   Fixed at the template, not per post.
2. Any relief, allowance or refund figure that a source states as a maximum must
   be published as a maximum. "Up to" is not optional.
3. Romanian copy was weaker than English on three separate points in the same
   batch. Romanian is the primary audience and should be reviewed first, not
   treated as a translation of the English.
4. `marriage-allowance` is only correct while 2022/23 remains in date: from
   6 April 2027 the earliest backdatable year becomes 2023/24. `plati-in-avans`
   dates out after 31 July 2027. Both persist on Pinterest and Google Business,
   so both need retiring or updating rather than simply being published.
5. Proposed editorial rule: Ola publishes on tax, and on the tax consequences of
   a benefit, but does not publish benefit eligibility criteria. That rule alone
   would have pre-empted the Winter Fuel problem at no cost — the £35,000
   clawback is the part that belongs to this firm anyway.
