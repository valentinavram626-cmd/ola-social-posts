## RO — Instagram, Facebook, Threads, TikTok

Lucrezi pe cont propriu și faci munca de acasă? Scazi o sumă fixă pe lună din profit, în locul cheltuielilor curente ale casei: curățenie, căldură, lumină, apă la contor.

Suma, după orele de acasă într-o lună:
• 25-50 de ore → £10
• 51-100 de ore → £18
• 101 de ore sau mai multe → £26

Exemplu: 60 de ore pe lună, tot anul. 12 × £18 = £216 scăzuți din profit. Se numără și luna începută, nu doar lunile întregi.

Se numără orice oră lucrată în casă numai pentru afacere, nu doar producția sau actele. Și orele lucrate acasă de angajații tăi, dar o oră o singură dată.

Sub 25 de ore într-o lună nu iei nimic pe luna aceea, dar celelalte luni se iau normal: e o condiție pe lună, nu pe persoană. Se cer 25 de ore sau mai multe: o lună cu exact 25 intră. O lună plină poate ieși £26 și una liniștită £10, în același an.

Cine NU poate folosi suma fixă:
• firmele Ltd, și o asociere în care intră o firmă. Interdicția e pe firmă, nu pe om: dacă directorul are și o activitate pe cont propriu, în aceea poate
• cine cere trading allowance — cu scutirea aceea nu mai deduci nicio altă cheltuială
• angajații și directorii, pentru munca de acasă din slujbă. Acolo e altă regulă: din 6 aprilie 2026 angajatul nu mai cere singur cheltuielile casei, dar plata de la angajator rămâne netaxată

Nu acoperă tot: chiria, council tax, asigurarea, reparațiile, telefonul și internetul se cer separat, cu partea de muncă și cu documente. La mulți trec de £10-£26.

Suma fixă e opțională: dacă iese mai bine pe cheltuieli reale, alegi aceea.

Se trece în declarația de Self Assessment sau, dacă ești în Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, în raportări trimestriale și declarația finală. În tot Regatul Unit, nimic nu vine automat, iar sumele se pot schimba.

## EN — LinkedIn  (postat ca document PDF, nu ca imagini multiple)

Self-employed with the work happening at home: HMRC's simplified expenses take a flat amount off profit each month in place of apportioning the home's running costs — cleaning, heat and light and metered water.

The rates, by hours of qualifying work at home a month:
• 25 to 50 hours → £10
• 51 to 100 hours → £18
• 101 hours or more → £26

Worked: 60 hours a month at home for a full year is 12 × £18 = £216 off profit. A month, or part of a month, carries the full band, so a trade starting mid-month is not pro-rated. The rates are current rather than permanent.

Qualifying work is any work done in the home wholly and exclusively for the trade — not only producing, record-keeping and marketing. Work done there by the trader's own employees counts too, with any one hour taken into account once.

Assessed month by month, so a busy month can reach £26 and a quiet one £10 in the same year. A month under 25 hours produces no amount for that month, and every other month in the period is claimed as normal. The floor is 25 hours or more, so exactly 25 hours qualifies.

Who cannot use it:
• limited companies. GOV.UK is explicit: "Simplified expenses cannot be used by limited companies or business partnerships involving a limited company". That excludes the entity whose profits are computed, not a person by job title — a director who also carries on a trade in their own name can use the flat rate in that trade
• a partnership with a company as a partner — only partnerships comprising solely individual partners qualify
• anyone claiming the trading allowance, which rules out deducting any other expenses or allowances
• employees and directors, for the use of home in the employment. That sits in the employment rules, not here — and since 6 April 2026 an employee can no longer deduct additional household expenses themselves, so what remains is a homeworking payment from the employer, which is still exempt

What it does not stand in for: rent or mortgage interest, council tax, insurance, general repairs and water rates are still apportioned to business use and still need the evidence, as do telephone and internet. For most home-based traders those exceed £10 to £26 a month, and they decide whether actual costs beat it.

Using the flat rate is optional; the choice is made on the figures.

UK-wide, claimed on the Self Assessment return — or, for a trader already in Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, through the quarterly updates and the final declaration.

Worth checking which method gives the better result before January.

## Surse primare

- The flat rates, by hours of business use a month: "25 to 50" is "£10", "51 to 100" is "£18", "101 and more" is "£26". Also verbatim: "You can only use simplified expenses if you work for 25 hours or more a month from home", "This means you do not have to work out the proportion of personal and business use for your home", and "The flat rate does not include telephone or internet expenses"
  https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-simplified-expenses/working-from-home  (citit 2026-11-07)
- Who may and may not use simplified expenses, verbatim: "Simplified expenses cannot be used by limited companies or business partnerships involving a limited company" — they are for sole traders and "business partnerships that have no companies as partners". The flat rates cover some vehicles, working from home and living in your business premises, and "You must calculate all other expenses by working out the actual costs". Records needed: the hours worked from home, with the amount included in total expenses on the Self Assessment return
  https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-simplified-expenses  (citit 2026-11-07)
- HMRC Business Income Manual BIM75010, which states the same three bands and amounts and adds what the guidance page does not: the deduction "is only available if these hours exceed 25 per month"; the deduction for a period is the total of the monthly rates "for each month, or part of a month"; "Use of the flat rate deduction is optional" and is available whether or not the cash basis is used; more than one home is "treated as a single home for the purposes of calculating hours worked"; and "Only partnerships comprising solely individual partners can claim this simplified expenses". It also records that the flat rate does not bar a separate claim for a business share of fixed costs such as council tax, insurance and mortgage interest, nor a separate claim for telephone and broadband
  https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim75010  (citit 2026-11-07)
