## RO — Instagram, Facebook, Threads, TikTok

Nu toată lumea trebuie să depună declarație la HMRC. Se întâmplă să depună cine nu trebuie, și să afle târziu cine trebuia.

Dacă ai doar salariu sau pensie de la job, taxa se ia prin PAYE înainte să fii plătit și de regulă nu depui nimic. Pensia de stat nu se taxează însă înainte de plată: HMRC ia taxa din codul fiscal pe alt venit, iar dacă nu poate, îți trimite după încheierea anului o Simple Assessment — nu e declarație, dar o verifici și o plătești.

Depui dacă, în anul fiscal trecut:
• ai lucrat pe cont propriu și ai încasat peste £1.000 — cât ai încasat, nu cât îți rămâne
• ai fost partener într-un business
• ai avut Capital Gains Tax de plătit
• ai avut taxa pe Child Benefit (venit net ajustat peste £60.000) și nu se ia prin PAYE

Poate intra și venitul netaxat: chirii, bacșișuri, dobânzi, dividende, bani din altă țară.

Scrisoarea prin care HMRC îți cere declarația se depune chiar dacă nu datorezi nimic. Dar nu e definitivă: dacă nu mai ai de ce să depui, spune-i HMRC — poate fi retrasă.

Anunțarea până pe 5 octombrie nu e doar pentru cine intră prima dată: e și pentru cine a mai depus, dar nu a avut nevoie de declarație anul trecut. Pentru 2025/26 a fost 5 octombrie 2026. L-ai ratat? Înregistrează-te acum: poți lua o penalizare, nu o amendă fixă — se calculează după comportament, iar manualul HMRC dă minim zero dacă anunți din proprie inițiativă, fără intenție, în 12 luni.

Termene: înregistrat la timp, depui online până pe 31 ianuarie 2027. Înregistrat abia acum, HMRC îți scrie alt termen de depunere, la 3 luni de la scrisoare — dar taxa se plătește tot până pe 31 ianuarie 2027.

GOV.UK nu face distincție pe națiuni.

## EN — LinkedIn  (postat ca document PDF, nu ca imagini multiple)

Not everyone has to file a Self Assessment return. Some file who need not, and some find out too late that they had to.

If your only income is wages or a workplace pension, the tax comes off through PAYE before you are paid, and no return is normally due. The State Pension is the exception people miss: GOV.UK says "tax is not taken off before you get it". HMRC usually collects it through the tax code on other income, and where it cannot, issues a Simple Assessment after the year end — a bill to check and pay, which GOV.UK says is "not the same as making a Self Assessment tax return".

You must send a return if, in the last tax year:
• you were self-employed as a sole trader and earned more than £1,000 — what came in, before anything you can claim tax relief on
• you were a partner in a business partnership
• you had to pay Capital Gains Tax on something you sold that had increased in value
• you had to pay the High Income Child Benefit Charge and do not pay it through PAYE

Untaxed income can bring you in as well: rent from property or land, tips and commission, savings interest, dividends, foreign income, and any taxable UK income if you are not UK resident.

Who does NOT normally file: anyone whose only income is wages or a workplace pension, where PAYE has already taken the tax. The Child Benefit charge still bites on wages alone — and so does being an off-payroll worker repaying a student or postgraduate loan.

Two more, both missed regularly:
• A notice to file is answered even where nothing is owed — but it is not unconditional. HMRC can withdraw it (SAM120116, letter SA832), and GOV.UK tells anyone who no longer needs to send a return to contact HMRC.
• Telling HMRC by 5 October is not only for first-timers. It also covers anyone who registered before but did not need to send a return for the previous tax year — the returning filer with a gap year. For 2025/26 the date was 5 October 2026. Miss it and GOV.UK says you "could get a penalty" — a penalty, not a fixed fine: it is behaviour-based, and HMRC's manual sets the minimum at nil where the disclosure is unprompted, non-deliberate and made within 12 months.

Deadlines. Registered on time, the 2025/26 online return is due by 31 January 2027. Register now instead and HMRC writes with a different filing deadline, three months from its letter — but you must still pay what you owe by 31 January 2027.

GOV.UK draws no distinction by nation on who has to file.

## Surse primare

- Who must send a return: sole trader earning more than £1,000 before relief; partner in a business partnership; Capital Gains Tax to pay; High Income Child Benefit Charge not paid through PAYE; and the untaxed income list — rent, tips and commission, savings interest, dividends, foreign income
  https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/who-must-send-a-tax-return  (citit 2026-11-09)
- Tax is usually deducted automatically from wages and pensions; people and businesses with other income must report it in a Self Assessment return; tell HMRC by 5 October if you need to complete a return for the previous year
  https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns  (citit 2026-11-09)
- Most people pay Income Tax through PAYE — the employer or pension provider takes Income Tax before paying wages or a pension
  https://www.gov.uk/income-tax/how-you-pay-income-tax  (citit 2026-11-09)
- High Income Child Benefit Charge: applies where adjusted net income is over £60,000 for tax years from 2024 to 2025; where both partners are over it, whoever has the higher income pays; it must go through Self Assessment where a return is needed for another reason or the payment falls after 31 January following the tax year, and can otherwise be paid through PAYE
  https://www.gov.uk/child-benefit-tax-charge  (citit 2026-11-09)
- State Pension: "Your State Pension is taxable income, but tax is not taken off before you get it"; HMRC "will usually change your tax code so the tax due is collected from your wages or other pensions", and where it cannot, "we may send you a Simple Assessment tax calculation after the end of the tax year"
  https://www.gov.uk/guidance/how-your-state-pension-is-taxed  (citit 2026-11-09)
- Simple Assessment: "Simple Assessment is not the same as making a Self Assessment tax return"; issued where tax could not be collected through the tax code, where £3,000 or more is owed, or where tax is due on the State Pension; the bill says how much is owed, how it was worked out and how to pay
  https://www.gov.uk/understand-simple-assessment  (citit 2026-11-09)
- The 5 October duty is not limited to first-timers: it covers anyone who has not sent a return before AND anyone who "registered before but did not need to send a tax return for the tax year 2024 to 2025"; GOV.UK's word for the consequence is a penalty — "If you tell HMRC after 5 October 2026, you could get a penalty"
  https://www.gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment  (citit 2026-11-09)
- Deadlines for 2025/26: online return and payment both 11:59pm on 31 January 2027; register late and HMRC "will send you a letter or email with a different deadline to send your tax return by", which "will be 3 months from the date on the letter or email", while "You must still pay the tax you owe by 11:59pm on 31 January 2027"
  https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/deadlines  (citit 2026-11-09)
- A return request is not unconditional: "If you believe you no longer need to send a return, you'll need to" contact HMRC and explain
  https://www.gov.uk/check-if-you-need-tax-return  (citit 2026-11-09)
- Withdrawing a notice to file: the action guide for a return "issued incorrectly or no longer required to be completed", and SEES Letter SA832 (Withdrawal of a Notice to file), which states the original payment due dates still apply. Where an SA criterion is satisfied the return must still be completed
  https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/self-assessment-manual/sam120116  (citit 2026-11-09)
- Failure-to-notify penalties are behaviour-based, not fixed: "The minimum penalty is nil where a disclosure is unprompted and non-deliberate", for a disclosure made less than 12 months after the tax first became unpaid
  https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/compliance-handbook/ch73180  (citit 2026-11-09)
