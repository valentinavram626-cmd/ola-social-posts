## RO — Instagram, Facebook, Threads, TikTok

Ai ajuns la destinație cu peste 3 ore întârziere? Compania îți poate datora bani.

Suma e stabilită prin lege, de persoană, oricât ai dat pe bilet. Nu se plătește automat: o ceri direct de la companie.

Cât, după distanță:
• sub 1.500 km — £220
• între 1.500 și 3.500 km — £350
• peste 3.500 km — £260 la 3–4 ore, £520 la peste 4 ore

Contează ora sosirii la destinație, nu a plecării programate. Distanța se măsoară între primul și ultimul aeroport din rezervare.

Separat de compensație, la întârziere lungă ai dreptul la îngrijire, indiferent de vină: mâncare și băutură, două telefoane sau e-mailuri, iar peste noapte cazare și transport. De la plecarea programată: 2 ore sub 1.500 km, 3 până la 3.500 km, 4 peste. Dacă plătești tu, păstrează bonurile.

La cel puțin 5 ore întârziere poți renunța la zbor și ceri banii pe partea nefolosită.

Când NU primești bani:
• sub 3 ore la destinație — nicio compensație
• când compania arată un motiv extraordinar: vreme care nu permite zborul în siguranță — nu doar vreme extremă — sau grevă fără legătură cu compania, la aeroport ori la controlul traficului aerian. Compania decide întâi; decizia se poate contesta.
• două rezervări separate legate de tine: nu ai drept legal la îngrijire sau compensație peste punctul de legătură

Ce intră: orice zbor care pleacă dintr-un aeroport din UK, cu orice companie. Spre UK, zborurile operate de companii din UK sau din UE — deci și cele din România cu companii din UE.

Termene: acțiune în instanță în 6 ani de la incident în Anglia și Țara Galilor, 5 în Scoția. Fără răspuns în 8 săptămâni sau cu un răspuns care nu-ți convine, mergi la organismul ADR al companiei; dacă nu e membru în unul aprobat, la autoritatea aviatică — care nu preia plângeri cu mai puțin de un an rămas. Rezident în Irlanda de Nord, zbor din sau spre NI: Consumer Council for Northern Ireland.

Nu știi dacă zborul tău intră?

## EN — LinkedIn  (postat ca document PDF, nu ca imagini multiple)

Landed more than three hours late? Your airline may owe a sum set by law — £220 to £520 a person, whatever you paid for the ticket. The CAA says it "is not paid automatically" — you claim it from the airline.

How much, by distance:
• under 1,500km — £220
• 1,500km to 3,500km — £350
• over 3,500km — £260 at three to four hours late, £520 beyond four

What counts is when you reached your destination airport, not when you were due to depart. Distance is the Great Circle Route between the first and last airports booked.

Separately, a long delay triggers a right to care whatever the cause: food and drink, two calls or emails, and accommodation with transport overnight. Those hours run from the scheduled departure time: two under 1,500km, three to 3,500km, four beyond. Paying yourself, keep itemised receipts — the CAA notes airlines are unlikely to treat alcohol or luxury hotels as reasonable.

At a delay of at least five hours you can decline to fly and claim a refund: parts not flown, any flown part that no longer serves your travel plan, and a flight back to where you started.

When you get NOTHING:
• under three hours late at the destination — no compensation
• where the airline proves an extraordinary cause unavoidable even with all reasonable measures. The CAA's weather test is weather "incompatible with the safe operation of the flight", not extreme weather, so ordinary fog or a snow shower can qualify; and only strikes "unrelated to the airline such as airport staff, ground handlers, air traffic control or border force" count — a strike by the airline's own crew does not. The airline decides first; that can be challenged.
• two separate bookings self-connected by you: no statutory right to care or compensation across the join; each flight's own entitlements still apply

What is covered: every flight departing a UK airport, on any airline. Into the UK, flights operated by a UK or EU airline. What puts a flight into the UK outside UK261 is its carrier, not its departure country: an airline that is neither UK nor EU, from anywhere.

If the airline says no, complain in writing. With no reply within eight weeks, or a reply you are not satisfied with, escalate: to the airline's approved ADR body, and to the CAA's passenger advice and complaints team only where the airline or airport is not in one. Residents of Northern Ireland, on a flight in or out of NI, go to the Consumer Council for Northern Ireland.

Court claims: 6 years from the incident in England and Wales, 5 in Scotland and, under the Limitation (NI) Order 1989, 6 in Northern Ireland. The CAA will not handle a complaint with less than a year of that left.

## Surse primare

- Delay compensation: £220 under 1,500km; £350 between 1,500km and 3,500km; £260 over 3,500km arriving three to four hours late and £520 arriving more than four hours late; you must arrive more than three hours late at the destination airport; amounts are fixed and not linked to the ticket price; compensation is not paid automatically and is claimed from the airline; no compensation where the airline considers the delay caused by extraordinary circumstances
  https://www.caa.co.uk/air-passengers/travel-problems-and-rights/flight-delays-and-cancellations/delays/  (citit 2026-11-09)
- What is likely to be an extraordinary circumstance, verbatim: "Weather conditions incompatible with the safe operation of the flight"; "Strikes (unrelated to the airline such as airport staff, ground handlers, air traffic control or border force)"; acts of terrorism or sabotage; security risks; political or civil unrest; hidden manufacturing defects. The test is not that the weather be extreme but that it be incompatible with safe operation, and a strike by the airline's own staff is not in the list
  https://www.caa.co.uk/air-passengers/travel-problems-and-rights/travel-complaints/making-a-claim/am-i-entitled-to-compensation/  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Retained UK261 Article 6(1)(iii): where the delay is at least five hours, the carrier must offer the assistance in Article 8(1)(a). "At least five hours" includes a delay of exactly five hours, where the CAA's delays page says "more than five hours"; Article 6(3), inserted 14 December 2023, is the arrival-based three-hour compensation right
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/261/article/6  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Retained UK261 Article 8(1)(a): reimbursement within seven days of the full cost of the ticket for "the part or parts of the journey not made", and for parts already made where the flight "is no longer serving any purpose in relation to the passenger's original travel plan", together with "a return flight to the first point of departure" at the earliest opportunity
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/261/article/8  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Where a complaint goes: the CAA's passenger advice and complaints team "is only likely to consider your complaint if the airline or airport you've complained to" "is not a member of an approved ADR body", and only once you have complained in writing and are "not satisfied with the outcome or have not received a reply within 8 weeks". The Consumer Council for Northern Ireland sits under the heading "Residents of Northern Ireland about a flight in or out of NI", whose text is "Complaints relating to this should be addressed to the Consumer Council for Northern Ireland" — two conditions, residence and an NI flight, not one. The page states court limits for England and Wales (6 years) and Scotland (5 years) and gives no figure for Northern Ireland, which is silence and not the absence of a limit (see the Limitation (Northern Ireland) Order 1989 below)
  https://www.caa.co.uk/air-passengers/travel-problems-and-rights/travel-complaints/how-the-caa-can-help/  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Which flights UK261 covers, by departure point, arrival point and airline: every flight departing the UK is inside it on any carrier; EU to UK is inside it on a UK or Community carrier; third country to UK on a third-country carrier is the row that is outside it
  https://www.caa.co.uk/media/jn3npj1h/uk-261-annex-1.pdf  (citit 2026-11-09)
- Northern Ireland's court time limit, which the CAA's page does not give. Article 4 of the Limitation (Northern Ireland) Order 1989, "Time limit: actions founded on contract, etc.", verbatim: "Subject to Articles 5, 7 and 9, the following actions may not be brought after the expiration of six years from the date on which the cause of action accrued", and the list includes "(a) an action founded on simple contract" and "(d) an action to recover any sum recoverable by virtue of any statutory provision". A UK261 claim is both, so the Northern Ireland limit is six years
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisi/1989/1339/article/4/made  (citit 2026-11-10)
- The latest revised text of Part II of the Limitation (Northern Ireland) Order 1989, read to confirm the six years in article 4 has not been amended: the lead-in still reads "may not be brought after the expiration of six years from the date on which the cause of action accrued", with no outstanding effects noted for the Part
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nisi/1989/1339/part/II/data.pdf  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Retained UK261 Article 3(1), which decides what is covered by the OPERATING CARRIER and not by the country of departure. Verbatim: "(a) to passengers departing from an airport located in the United Kingdom"; "(b) to passengers departing from an airport located in a country other than the United Kingdom to an airport situated in— (i) the United Kingdom if the operating air carrier of the flight concerned is a Community carrier or a UK air carrier". So an inbound flight is outside UK261 whenever the operating carrier is neither UK nor EU, whatever country it left — including a flight from the EU
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/261/article/3  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Retained UK261 Article 6(1) and 6(4), read again for the event the care hours are measured from and for what the airline has to show. 6(1) verbatim: "When an operating air carrier reasonably expects a flight to be delayed beyond its scheduled time of departure" — "for two hours or more in the case of flights of 1500 kilometres or less", "for three hours or more in the case of all flights between 1500 and 3500 kilometres", "for four hours or more in the case of all flights not falling under (a) or (b)". So the 2/3/4-hour care thresholds run from SCHEDULED DEPARTURE, where the three-hour compensation threshold in 6(3) runs from arrival at the final destination. 6(3) verbatim: "In case of a delay of three hours or more in reaching the final destination, the passengers concerned have the right to compensation laid down in Article 7" — "three hours or more" includes exactly three hours, where the CAA's consumer page says "more than three hours late". 6(4) verbatim: the carrier is not obliged to pay "if it can prove that the delay is caused by extraordinary circumstances which could not have been avoided even if all reasonable measures had been taken"
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/261/article/6  (citit 2026-11-10)
- The CAA delays page read again for three things the earlier reading did not record. Care, verbatim: "You are entitled to care if your flight is delayed beyond its scheduled departure time by:" "at least two hours for flights under 1,500km", "at least three hours for flights between 1,500km and 3,500km", "at least four hours for flights over 3,500km". Automatic payment, verbatim: "Compensation is not paid automatically and you will need to claim this directly from your airline in the first instance" — "not", not "never". Self-transfer, verbatim: where a journey "includes two or more separate flight bookings (sometimes called 'self-transfer')" you "do not have a statutory right to care, compensation or transport to your last destination", followed by the saving clause "However, the relevant entitlements for each individual flight still apply."
  https://www.caa.co.uk/air-passengers/travel-problems-and-rights/flight-delays-and-cancellations/delays/  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Time limit to issue a court claim: the legal limit to issue claims at Court is 6 years from the date of the incident in England and Wales and 5 years in Scotland; the CAA will not handle complaints where there is less than a year to take legal action
  https://www.caa.co.uk/air-passengers/travel-problems-and-rights/travel-complaints/how-the-caa-can-help/  (citit 2026-11-09)
