## RO — Instagram, Facebook, Threads, TikTok

Dacă ești însărcinată sau ai un copil sub 4 ani și familia ia Universal Credit, s-ar putea să ai dreptul la bani pe un card, în fiecare săptămână, numai pentru mâncare.

Se cheamă NHS Healthy Start. Nu e un împrumut și nu se scade din altceva.

Cât intră:
— £4,65 pe săptămână din a 10-a săptămână de sarcină
— £9,30 pe săptămână pentru un copil de la naștere până la 1 an
— £4,65 pe săptămână pentru fiecare copil între 1 și 4 ani

Banii intră pe card o dată la 4 săptămâni. Sumele au crescut în septembrie 2026: dacă ai văzut £4,25 și £8,50, sunt vechi.

Pe ce se dau: lapte de vacă simplu, fructe și legume proaspete, congelate sau la conservă, fasole, linte, mazăre și năut, și lapte praf de început. Nu merge pe fructe în sirop, sucuri, smoothie-uri sau conserve cu sare, zahăr sau sos. Primești și vitamine gratuite.

Cum intri: cu Universal Credit, dacă familia câștigă £408 pe lună sau mai puțin în mână, din muncă. Sau cu Pension Credit care include partea pentru copil — acolo cererea se face la telefon sau pe email, nu online.

Cine NU îl primește:
— cine stă în Scoția. Acolo e Best Start Foods, cu alte sume, alte condiții, și se oprește la 3 ani, nu la 4;
— familiile care câștigă peste £408 pe lună în mână;
— se oprește când copilul face 4 ani;
— se oprește și dacă nu mai iei ajutorul care ți-a dat dreptul. NHS verifică o dată la 4 săptămâni, iar dacă nu mai ești eligibil mai ai 16 săptămâni să cheltui ce e pe card.

Capcana pe care nu o știe aproape nimeni: dacă nu folosești cardul timp de 16 săptămâni, cererea se închide și banii rămași se iau înapoi.

O singură cerere pe gospodărie. Și trebuie cerută — nu vine automat, oricât ai fi de îndreptățit.

## EN — LinkedIn  (postat ca document PDF, nu ca imagini multiple)

If you are pregnant or have a child under 4 and the household is on Universal Credit, there may be money on a card for you every week, for food alone.

It is called NHS Healthy Start. It is not a loan and it is not deducted from anything else.

What goes on the card:
— £4.65 a week from the 10th week of pregnancy
— £9.30 a week for a child from birth to 1 year old
— £4.65 a week for each child aged 1 to 4

The card is topped up every four weeks. The rates rose in September 2026 — the £4.25 and £8.50 figures still circulating are out of date.

What it buys: plain liquid cow's milk, fresh, frozen or tinned fruit and vegetables, fresh, dried or tinned pulses, and first infant formula based on cow's milk. Not fruit in syrup, juices, smoothies, or tins with added salt, sugar or sauce. Free vitamins come with it.

How you qualify: Universal Credit, where the family takes home £408 a month or less from employment. Or Pension Credit that includes the child addition, in which case the application is made by phone or email rather than online.

Who does not get it:
— anyone in Scotland. There it is Best Start Foods, on different amounts and different rules, and it stops at 3 rather than 4
— a household taking home more than £408 a month
— it stops when the child turns 4
— it stops if the qualifying benefit stops. NHSBSA checks with the benefits agencies every four weeks, and if eligibility ends there are 16 weeks to spend what is on the card

The trap almost nobody knows about: if the card is not used for more than 16 weeks the claim is closed and the remaining funds are removed. Use it at least every few weeks.

One active claim per household. And it has to be claimed — nothing arrives automatically, however clearly you qualify.

**Titlu:** NHS Healthy Start: weekly money on a card for food

NHS Healthy Start gives families in England, Wales and Northern Ireland a prepaid card for milk, fruit, vegetables, pulses and first infant formula, plus free vitamins. £4.65 a week from the 10th week of pregnancy, £9.30 a week for a child under 1, and £4.65 a week for a child aged 1 to 4. You qualify on Universal Credit with family take-home pay of £408 a month or less, or on Pension Credit including the child addition. Not available in Scotland, which runs Best Start Foods. It stops at age 4, and it has to be claimed.

- NHS Healthy Start, what you'll get and how to shop — "You'll get: £4.65 each week of your pregnancy from the 10th week; £9.30 each week for children from birth to 1 year old; £4.65 each week for children between 1 and 4 years old." 'Your money will stop when your child is 4 years old, or if you do not get benefits that make you eligible.' The card cannot be used online, outside the UK, on a phone wallet app, to withdraw cash or to get cashback.
  https://www.healthystart.nhs.uk/what-youll-get-and-how-to-shop/  (citit 2026-11-12)
- NHS Healthy Start, how to apply — "You can apply online if: you're more than 10 weeks pregnant or have at least one child under 4 years old; your family's monthly 'take-home pay' (also called 'earned income') is £408 or less from employment; you get Universal Credit." And: 'You can apply by email or phone if you're more than 10 weeks pregnant, or have at least one child under 4 years old, and get Pension Credit (which includes the child addition).' A person under 18 and more than 10 weeks pregnant can apply by email or phone without any qualifying benefit, but 'After your baby is born, you will only continue to qualify for Healthy Start if you get the qualifying benefits.'
  https://www.healthystart.nhs.uk/how-to-apply/  (citit 2026-11-12)
- NHS Healthy Start, scheme rules — the card 'will be topped up every 4 weeks'. 'Once you have successfully applied and we've confirmed your eligibility, you become a beneficiary of the NHS Healthy Start scheme' — there is no auto-enrolment. 'We share information with the benefits agencies every four weeks to check that you're still eligible... If our checks show that you're no longer eligible, your payments will stop and you'll have 16 weeks to spend any remaining funds.' 'Make sure you use your card at least once every 16 weeks.' 'There should not be more than one active claim per household.' What may be bought: plain liquid cow's milk, fresh, frozen and tinned fruit and vegetables, fresh, dried and tinned pulses, and first infant formula based on cow's milk.
  https://www.healthystart.nhs.uk/scheme-rules/  (citit 2026-11-12)
