## RO — Instagram, Facebook, Threads, TikTok

În pensia ta de la job pune bani și angajatorul. Dacă ieși, partea lui nu se înlocuiește cu nimic.

La un loc de muncă, în pensie intră minimum 8% din câștigul care se ia în calcul: 3% pune angajatorul și 5% pui tu. Deci 3% nu e 3% din cei 8% — e 3% din câștig. Dacă plătești Income Tax, o parte din cei 5% vine ca scutire, nu din buzunar.

Cine intră automat, fără să ceară:
• ești «worker» — intră și munca prin agenție
• ai între 22 de ani și vârsta de pensionare de stat
• câștigi cel puțin £10.000 pe an
• lucrezi de obicei în UK

Procentele nu merg pe tot salariul: în majoritatea schemelor automate, doar pe câștigul dintre £6.240 și £50.270 pe an, înainte de taxe. Cifre 2026/27.

Dacă nu bifezi toate condițiile, nu te înscrie nimeni automat. Între 16 și 74 de ani de regulă poți cere tu să intri și angajatorul nu te poate refuza — dar la £6.240 pe an sau mai puțin (£520 pe lună) nu e obligat să pună și el bani. Trebuie totuși să-ți ia contribuția din salariu și s-o pună în schemă.

GOV.UK mai listează cazuri în care angajatorul de regulă nu e obligat să te înscrie, chiar dacă le bifezi: ți-ai dat demisia sau ai primit preaviz, ai luat deja o pensie aranjată de angajator, ai ieșit din schemă cu peste 12 luni înainte de data de pornire, ești într-un limited liability partnership, sau ești director fără contract de muncă și ai cel puțin un alt angajat în firmă.

The Pensions Regulator adaugă: un director unic, fără alt personal, nu are deloc obligația de înscriere.

Ieși în prima lună după înscriere — primești banii înapoi. Mai târziu, de regulă rămân în pensie până la pensionare.

Reînscrierea merge după data angajatorului, nu după a ta: cam din 3 în 3 ani sunt verificați cei ieșiți din schemă și repuși doar cei care mai îndeplinesc condițiile. Primesc o scrisoare.

GOV.UK nu face distincție pe națiuni.

Nu știi dacă ești înscris?

## EN — LinkedIn  (postat ca document PDF, nu ca imagini multiple)

Your employer puts money into your workplace pension too. Opt out and that part is not replaced.

In a UK job, at least 8% of your qualifying earnings goes into a pension: 3% of those earnings from the employer and 5% from you. So the 3% is three points of your earnings, not 3% of the 8%. And if you pay Income Tax, part of your 5% arrives as tax relief rather than out of your pocket.

Who is enrolled automatically, without asking:
• classed as a ‘worker’ — wider than payroll: agency and casual work can count
• aged between 22 and State Pension age
• earning at least £10,000 a year
• ordinarily working in the UK

In most automatic enrolment schemes the percentages apply to earnings between £6,240 and £50,270 a year before tax, not to your whole wage. £10,000, £6,240 and £50,270 are the 2026/27 figures.

Miss any of those and nobody enrols you. From 16 to 74 you can usually still join and your employer cannot refuse — and above £6,240 a year it must then contribute too. At £6,240 or less (£520 a month, £120 a week) it need not contribute at all. It must still deduct your own contributions and pay them into the scheme.

The Pensions Regulator adds the case GOV.UK does not: a sole director with no other staff has no automatic enrolment duty at all.

GOV.UK also lists cases where an employer usually need not enrol someone even on the right age and pay: notice of leaving already given or received, a pension already taken that the employer arranged, a winding up lump sum from a closed scheme where you then left and were re-employed by the same employer within 12 months of getting it, having opted out more than 12 months before the staging date, an EU cross-border scheme, a limited liability partnership, and a director without an employment contract who employs at least one other person.

Leave within a month of being added and you get back what you have paid in. Later, it usually stays in the pension until you retire.

Usually stated wrongly: re-enrolment does not run from the date you joined. The regulator puts it roughly every three years from the employer's own duties start date, then from its last re-enrolment date, on a day the employer picks from a six-month window around that anniversary. Only staff who opted out, left the scheme or cut their contributions are assessed then, and only those aged 22 to State Pension age earning over £10,000 go back in. The employer must write to each within six weeks. GOV.UK's plain-English page says it runs from the date you first enrolled; the regulator's detailed guidance is the operative rule.

GOV.UK draws no distinction by nation.

## Surse primare

- Automatic enrolment conditions, all four: you are 'classed as a worker', aged between 22 and State Pension age, earn at least £10,000 a year and usually ('ordinarily') work in the UK. The list of cases where an employer usually does not have to enrol you, read in full — the closed-scheme case is stated as a one-off 'winding up lump sum' from a closed workplace scheme AND leaving and rejoining the same job within 12 months of getting it, and the director case as a director 'without an employment contract' who 'employs at least one other person in your company'. 'You can usually still join their pension if you want to. Your employer cannot refuse.' No employer contribution required at £520 a month, £120 a week or £480 over 4 weeks or less. This page does not state the £6,240 figure.
  https://www.gov.uk/workplace-pensions/joining-a-workplace-pension  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Minimum total contribution 8% from April 2019, of which the employer pays at least 3% and the staff member 5%; 'in most automatic enrolment schemes' the percentages apply to earnings between £6,240 and £50,270 a year before tax. Tax relief is conditional: the government adds it where 'both of the following apply' — you pay Income Tax and you pay into a pension. Separately, if the scheme uses relief at source you still get an additional payment even if you pay no Income Tax.
  https://www.gov.uk/workplace-pensions/what-you-your-employer-and-the-government-pay  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Opting out within a month of being added returns the money already paid in; later 'you may not be able to get your payments refunded' and they 'usually stay in your pension until you retire'; a letter is sent when you are re-enrolled. NOTE — this plain-English page states the cycle as 'every 3 years (from the date you first enrolled)', which conflicts with The Pensions Regulator's detailed guidance 11 and its business-adviser guide. The regulator's own guidance is the operative rule and is what the cards and captions follow: the date is the employer's, not the individual's.
  https://www.gov.uk/workplace-pensions/if-you-want-to-leave-your-workplace-pension-scheme  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Cyclical automatic re-enrolment, detailed guidance 11: it 'occurs approximately every three years after an employer's staging date or duties start date', and the employer 'may choose this date from any date that falls within a six-month window, starting three months before the third anniversary' of that date; later cycles run from the third anniversary of the last re-enrolment date. The employer 'must assess only the workers who have opted out or voluntarily ceased active membership of a qualifying scheme'; workers aged 21 or under and workers at or over State Pension age on that date are excluded, and only an eligible jobholder aged at least 22 but under State Pension age with qualifying earnings above the trigger is put back in. Re-enrolment must be completed within six weeks of the re-enrolment date, and the worker then has a one-month opt-out period.
  https://www.thepensionsregulator.gov.uk/en/document-library/automatic-enrolment-detailed-guidance/11-automatic-re-enrolment-putting-workers-back-into-pension-scheme-membership  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Re-enrolment and re-declaration, business-adviser guide: 'We tell employers to use the third anniversary of their duties start date as their re-enrolment date', and 'for later re-enrolments, the date moves to the third anniversary of their most recent re-enrolment date'; they 'can choose any date within a six-month timeframe', 'starting three months before, and ending three months after their anniversary date'. Assess 'any staff who have left their pension scheme, or reduced their contributions into it'; those aged 22 to State Pension age earning over £10,000 a year 'must be put back into the pension scheme'. The employer 'must write to eligible staff individually, within six weeks of their re-enrolment date'.
  https://www.thepensionsregulator.gov.uk/en/business-advisers/automatic-enrolment-guide-for-business-advisers/re-enrolment-and-re-declaration  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Employer duties and defining the workforce, detailed guidance 1: a worker is someone working under a contract of employment, or under a contract 'to perform work or services personally and is not undertaking the work as part of their own business' — which 'may include agency workers'. Both jobholders and entitled workers are 'aged between 16 and 74', which is the band in which the right to opt in or to join applies. An entitled worker has no qualifying earnings in the pay reference period; for one, 'the employer will have to deduct contributions on behalf of the entitled worker and pay these into the scheme', while 'the employer does not have to pay into the scheme themselves, unless they choose to do so'. A non-eligible jobholder is a different case and is the one card 3 now states: aged 16 to 74 with qualifying earnings at or below the earnings trigger, not enrolled automatically, but 'if a non-eligible jobholder chooses to opt in' the employer 'must enrol the non-eligible jobholder into an automatic enrolment scheme' and 'the employer must pay employer contributions to the scheme' (paras 67-68). Qualifying earnings exist only above the lower limit, so the line between the two cases is £6,240, not £10,000.
  https://www.thepensionsregulator.gov.uk/en/document-library/automatic-enrolment-detailed-guidance/1-employer-duties-and-defining-the-workforce  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Pensions Act 2008 s.13(1): qualifying earnings in a 12-month pay reference period are the part of gross earnings that is '(a) more than £6,240, and (b) not more than £50,270'. 'More than', not 'at least': at exactly £6,240 there are no qualifying earnings, so no employer contribution duty arises — which is why card 3 and the captions now say '£6,240 or less' and not 'under £6,240'. Paragraph (a) was substituted with effect from 6 April 2020 and (b) from 6 April 2021.
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2008/30/section/13/data.html  (citit 2026-11-10)
- DWP review of the automatic enrolment earnings trigger and qualifying earnings band for 2026/27: the £10,000 earnings trigger 'should be retained for 2026 to 2027', the LEL 'will continue to be set at £6,240' and the UEL 'will continue to be set at £50,270'. On people earning between the lower earnings limit and the trigger — the group the review calls non-eligible jobholders — 'People in this group can opt-in to their employer's workplace pension and will receive a mandatory employer contribution'. This is the source for the new line on card 3.
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/review-of-the-automatic-enrolment-earnings-trigger-and-qualifying-earnings-band-for-202627/review-of-the-automatic-enrolment-earnings-trigger-and-qualifying-earnings-band-for-202627  (citit 2026-11-10)
- Automatic enrolment duties do not apply where the person is not an employer — including where you are a sole director of an organisation or business with no other staff. The Pensions Regulator is the regulator for automatic enrolment.
  https://www.thepensionsregulator.gov.uk/en/employers/what-if-i-dont-have-any-staff  (citit 2026-11-09)
