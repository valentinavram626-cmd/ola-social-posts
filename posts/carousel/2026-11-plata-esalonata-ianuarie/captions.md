Îți faci singur declarația la HMRC — Self Assessment? Nu aștepta ianuarie ca să începi să plătești taxele.

Planul se cheamă Budget Payment Plan. Pui deoparte o sumă săptămânal sau lunar, prin debit direct, iar banii se scad din ce ai de plătit. Tu alegi cât și cât de des. Îl pornești tu, din contul tău HMRC online: intri la Direct Debit și îl alegi. Îți trebuie contul Government Gateway și numărul UTR (10 cifre de la HMRC), iar pe gov.uk verifici singur dacă te încadrezi.

Aici se încurcă lumea: termenul nu se mută. Ce nu acoperă planul se plătește tot până pe 31 ianuarie 2027, la 23:59. Din 1 februarie curge dobânda, iar penalizarea vine mai târziu: 5% din taxa neplătită la 30 de zile, adică pe 2 martie 2027, apoi iar la 6 și la 12 luni. Deci dacă rămâi descoperit, plătește repede — până pe 2 martie nu s-a adăugat nicio penalizare de plată.

Dobânda HMRC pentru plata întârziată e 7,75% până pe 23 noiembrie 2026, apoi 8%.

Și 31 ianuarie nu e singura dată. Dacă ai plăți în avans — payments on account — pe 31 ianuarie plătești ce datorezi pe anul trecut plus prima dintre ele, iar a doua pe 31 iulie. Pui debitul la jumătate din cât ai nevoie și ianuarie iese bine, dar te prinde iulie. Socotește ambele termene.

Poți opri plățile până la 6 luni. Dacă ai pus mai mult, ceri diferența înapoi.

Cine NU îl poate folosi: trebuie să fii la zi cu plățile de la declarația trecută. Dacă ai sărit deja un termen de plată, drumul tău e altul: Time to Pay, eșalonarea pentru datoriile deja restante.

E doar pentru Self Assessment: nu TVA, nu taxele oprite din salariu (PAYE), nu impozitul pe firmă.

## EN — LinkedIn  (postat ca document PDF, nu ca imagini multiple)

If you fill in your own tax return — Self Assessment — and the January bill worries you, you do not have to wait until January to start paying it.

HMRC runs a Budget Payment Plan. You put an amount aside every week or every month by Direct Debit, and it comes off the bill when it arrives. You set it up yourself inside your HMRC online account: go to Direct Debit and choose the Budget Payment Plan option. Have your Government Gateway sign-in and your UTR — the 10-digit number HMRC gave you — to hand, and check on gov.uk first whether you can use the plan. You choose the amount and the frequency.

One thing to understand before you start, because this is where people come unstuck: the deadline does not move. If what you have put aside does not cover the whole bill, the rest is still due by 11:59pm on 31 January 2027. Interest runs from 1 February. The penalty comes later: 5% of the tax still unpaid 30 days after the deadline, which is 2 March 2027, and 5% again at six months and at twelve months. So if you are left short, paying quickly still counts — no late-payment penalty has been added before 2 March. If you are in credit, you can ask for a refund.

HMRC's late payment interest rate is 7.75% until 23 November 2026, and 8.00% from 24 November 2026.

And 31 January is not the only date. If you make payments on account, the 31 January payment is last year's balance plus the first of them, and the second falls due on 31 July. Set the Direct Debit at half of what you need and January will look fine while July catches you out. Work out both dates before you choose the amount.

What is flexible: you can pause the payments for up to six months if you need to.

Who cannot use it: you must be up to date with the payments from your last Self Assessment bill. GOV.UK is unambiguous — the plan only opens if you are not in arrears. If you have already missed a payment deadline, your route is the other one: Time to Pay, the arrangement for tax that is already overdue, where HMRC assesses your income and spending and decides what is affordable.

It is also Self Assessment only. It is not a route for VAT, for tax taken out of wages through PAYE, or for Corporation Tax.

UK-wide. Allow five working days for a first Direct Debit to be set up.

- Pay your Self Assessment tax bill: pay weekly or monthly — 'You can set up a Budget Payment plan to make weekly or monthly Direct Debit payments towards your next Self Assessment tax bill.' 'Your payments will be used against your next tax bill - this means you'll have less to pay at the payment deadline.' 'To set up a plan you must be up to date with any payments from your last Self Assessment tax bill.' 'You can pause payments for up to 6 months if you need to.' 'If the amount you've paid does not cover all of your next bill, you'll need to pay the difference. If you're in credit, you can ask for a refund.' And the signpost away: 'If you've missed your Self Assessment payment deadline or you cannot pay the amount you owe, you might be able to set up a payment plan (also known as a Time to Pay arrangement).'
  https://www.gov.uk/pay-self-assessment-tax-bill/pay-weekly-monthly  (citit 2026-11-12)
- If you cannot pay your tax bill on time: pay in instalments — the mirror-image signpost, 'If your bill is not overdue and you want to make weekly or monthly payments towards your next Self Assessment tax bill, you may be able to set up a Budget Payment Plan instead.' Time to Pay requires 'details of your income and spending' and 'HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) will check if a payment plan is affordable for you.'
  https://www.gov.uk/difficulties-paying-hmrc/pay-in-instalments  (citit 2026-11-12)
- Self Assessment tax returns: deadlines — 'The last tax year started on 6 April 2025 and ended on 5 April 2026.' 'You must submit your online tax return by 11:59pm on 31 January 2027 or you'll get a late filing penalty.' 'You need to pay your Self Assessment tax by 11:59pm on 31 January 2027 or you'll get a penalty.' 'There is a second payment deadline of 31 July if you make payments towards your bill - these are known as payments on account.'
  https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/deadlines  (citit 2026-11-12)
- HMRC interest rates for late and early payments, page last updated 6 November 2026 — the rate table gives 7.75% late payment and 2.75% repayment from 9 January 2026, and 8.00% late payment and 3.00% repayment from 24 November 2026. Late payment interest is 'set at base rate plus 4% from 6 April 2025'. So 7.75% is the rate in force until 23 November 2026 and 8.00% applies from 24 November 2026. The page's own summary box is written forward-looking and already quotes 8.00%, which is the rate from 24 November and not the rate today.
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rates-and-allowances-hmrc-interest-rates-for-late-and-early-payments/rates-and-allowances-hmrc-interest-rates  (citit 2026-11-12)
- Debt Management and Banking Manual DMBM200210 — lists 'Budget Payment Plans (BPPs) to enable customers to make regular payments towards their future Self Assessment (SA) liabilities' as a distinct plan type on the National Direct Debit System, separate from 'Time to Pay (TTP) payment plans'. The manual describes the Budget Payment Plan as being for future Self Assessment liabilities only.
  https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/debt-management-and-banking/dmbm200210  (citit 2026-11-12)
- Self Assessment tax returns: penalties — 'If you pay your tax late' you get 'penalties of 5% of the tax unpaid at: 30 days / 6 months / 12 months' and 'You'll also be charged interest on the amount owed.' So interest runs from 1 February but the first late-payment penalty is charged at 30 days, which for the 2025/26 bill is 2 March 2027 — the captions say so rather than implying a penalty lands on 1 February.
  https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/penalties  (citit 2026-11-12)
- Understand your Self Assessment tax bill: tax calculation (SA302) — the calculation 'does not include any: payments you have made into a Budget Payment Plan'. Worth knowing, because a taxpayer reading their SA302 sees the gross bill, not the bill net of what they have already put aside.
  https://www.gov.uk/understand-self-assessment-bill/tax-calculation-sa302  (citit 2026-11-12)
