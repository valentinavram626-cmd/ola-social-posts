## RO — Instagram, Facebook, Threads, TikTok

Ai emis factura în august, clientul plătește în noiembrie, iar TVA-ul l-ai dat lui HMRC în septembrie. Din banii tăi.

Asta e regula normală: TVA-ul se datorează la data facturii, nu la data încasării. Există o schemă care mută data: Cash Accounting Scheme. TVA-ul pe vânzări se datorează când intră banii.

Partea care decide dacă îți folosește: TVA-ul de pe cumpărături îl iei înapoi abia după ce îți plătești furnizorul.

Cum intri: ești deja înregistrat la TVA și te aștepți să vinzi £1,35 milioane sau mai puțin, fără TVA, în următoarele 12 luni. Nu există cerere și nu anunți pe nimeni: începi din prima zi a unei perioade de TVA, perioada pentru care depui declarația.

Un câștig care nu se vede: dacă un client nu te plătește niciodată, nu ai plătit TVA pe factura aceea.

Cui s-ar putea să nu-i folosească, zice HMRC: celui plătit pe loc, la vânzare; celui care cere de la HMRC mai mult TVA decât îi dă; și celui care facturează servicii lună de lună. Ai magazin sau fast-food și clientul plătește la casă? Schema nu îți mută nimic.

Iar dacă ești subcontractor în construcții și facturezi la reverse charge, nu e vorba de mai puțin folos: facturile alea sunt în afara schemei prin lege. Nu ai ce amâna, dar TVA-ul pe materiale tot îl iei mai târziu. Ieși în pierdere.

Cine NU poate intra: cine are declarații de TVA nedepuse sau plăți restante — dar dacă ai o înțelegere cu HMRC să-ți achiți restanțele, poți intra; și cine e pe Flat Rate Scheme.

Cine trebuie să iasă: când vânzările pe ultimele 12 luni trec de £1,6 milioane — dar dacă ai sărit pragul o singură dată, dintr-o vânzare mare, cum e o dubă sau un utilaj, s-ar putea să poți rămâne. Când ieși, datorezi tot TVA-ul rămas, chiar dacă nu ai fost plătit; îl poți raporta și plăti în 6 luni.

## EN — LinkedIn  (postat ca document PDF, nu ca imagini multiple)

You invoiced in August, the customer pays in November, and you handed the VAT to HMRC in September. Out of your own money.

That is the normal rule: VAT is due at the date of the invoice, not the date of payment. There is a scheme that moves the date — the Cash Accounting Scheme — under which VAT on sales is due when the money lands in your account.

The half that gets mentioned less, and the half that decides whether it suits you: it runs both ways. You reclaim VAT on purchases only once you have paid your supplier, not when their invoice arrives.

How you join: you are already registered for VAT, and you expect taxable supplies of £1.35 million or less, excluding VAT, over the next 12 months. It is not open to a business that is not VAT registered. There is no application and nobody to notify — you simply start on the first day of a VAT accounting period. It cannot be applied retrospectively; invoices from before you start stay on the normal rules.

A benefit that is easy to miss: if a customer never pays, you never paid VAT on that invoice. Under the normal rules you would have paid it, then waited six months from the due date to reclaim it, with the debt written off in your accounts first.

Who it may not help, in HMRC's words: a business usually paid as soon as it makes a sale, one that often reclaims more VAT than it pays, and one making continuous supplies of services. If you run a shop or a takeaway and the customer pays at the till, the scheme moves nothing for you.

A construction subcontractor invoicing under the domestic reverse charge is a harder case than that. Those supplies are outside the scheme as a matter of law, so there is no output tax to defer, while the input tax on materials still waits until the supplier has been paid. The scheme leaves that business worse off than the normal rules.

Who cannot join at all: anyone with VAT returns outstanding, or payments outstanding unless they have arranged with HMRC to clear the lot; anyone on the Flat Rate Scheme, which has its own cash-based method.

Who has to leave: once taxable supplies in the last 12 months exceed £1.6 million — though a one-off sale that takes you over, such as a capital asset, can let you stay on, if it has not happened before and you expect under £1.35 million over the next 12 months. On leaving, all outstanding VAT becomes due whether or not your customers have paid, and you can report and pay it over six months.

## Surse primare

- VAT Notice 731, Cash accounting scheme, section 1.2 (carries the force of law under regulations 56 to 65 of the VAT Regulations 1995) — 'The scheme allows you to account for VAT (output tax) on your sales based on payments you receive, rather than on tax invoices you issue.' And the other half: 'If you choose to use the scheme, you can only reclaim the VAT incurred on your purchases (input tax) once you pay your supplier.' Section 1.3 names who gains nothing: 'The scheme may not give you any benefit if you: are usually paid as soon as you make a sale / often reclaim more VAT than you pay / make continuous supplies of services'. Section 2.1 lists the joining conditions, including 'you expect the value of your taxable supplies in the next year will be £1.35 million or less', 'you've no VAT Returns outstanding', no VAT conviction, compounded proceedings or dishonest-evasion penalty in the last year, and no money owed to HMRC without an arrangement. Section 2.5: 'you may continue to use it until the annual value of your taxable supplies, including the disposal of stock and capital assets reaches £1.6 million... If you go over this figure, you'll have to leave the scheme at the end of your current tax period'. Section 2.8: 'You cannot use the Flat Rate Scheme with the Cash Accounting Scheme, but it does have its own cash based method.' Section 3.1, force of law: 'You cannot retrospectively apply the Cash Accounting Scheme to your business' and 'There's no need to apply to use the scheme'. Section 2.7 lists the transactions excluded even inside the scheme: lease purchase, hire purchase, conditional sale and credit sale; imports and acquisitions and goods removed from a customs warehouse or free zone; domestic reverse charge supplies; invoices where payment is not due in full within 6 months; and invoices issued in advance of the supply. Section 6.6: 'you do not have to account for VAT on bad debts.'
  https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-cash-accounting-scheme-notice-731  (citit 2026-11-12)
- Cash Accounting Scheme, the short guidance — 'With the Cash Accounting Scheme you: pay VAT on your sales when your customers pay you / reclaim VAT on your purchases when you have paid your supplier.' Eligibility: 'your estimated VAT taxable turnover is £1.35 million or less in the next 12 months'. 'You join at the beginning of a VAT accounting period. You do not have to tell HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) you use cash accounting.' 'You cannot use cash accounting if: you're not up to date with your VAT Returns or payments... you use the VAT Flat Rate Scheme'. On leaving: 'you must report and pay HMRC any outstanding VAT (whether your customers have paid you or not).'
  https://www.gov.uk/vat-cash-accounting-scheme  (citit 2026-11-12)
- The Value Added Tax Regulations 1995, regulation 58 — the statutory joining test, reg 58(1)(a): 'he has reasonable grounds for believing that the value of taxable supplies to be made by him in the period of one year then beginning will not exceed £1,350,000'. Reg 58(2) lists the excluded transactions, including supplies 'where a VAT invoice is issued and full payment of the amount shown on the invoice is not due for a period in excess of 6 months from the date of the issue of the invoice'. Reg 58(4): a person is not entitled to begin the scheme 'if the Commissioners consider it is necessary for the protection of the revenue'.
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1995/2518/regulation/58  (citit 2026-11-12)
- The Value Added Tax Regulations 1995, regulation 60 — the exit test: 'a person shall withdraw from the scheme immediately at the end of a prescribed accounting period of his if the value of taxable supplies made by him in the period of one year ending at the end of the prescribed accounting period in question has exceeded £1,600,000.'
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1995/2518/regulation/60  (citit 2026-11-12)
- VAT Cash Accounting Scheme Manual VCAS1100 and VCAS3050 — VCAS1100: the scheme 'gives automatic bad debt relief'. VCAS3050, on VAT groups: 'the turnover limit for cash accounting is applied to the group as a whole. If the turnover of the group as a whole is over £1,350,000, cash accounting cannot be used, even though the turnovers of the individual group members may be below this limit.' VCAS8000 confirms the £1,350,000 and £1,600,000 limits have been in force from 1 April 2007 to date.
  https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-cash-accounting-scheme/vcas1100  (citit 2026-11-12)
